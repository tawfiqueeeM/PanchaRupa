# Capacity and Operations

Explain the 10× monthly scale-up with a transparent production model

## Establish the comparison

Founder-reported output grew from 100 to 1,000 kg per month, a gain of 900 kg per month or 900%. Verify whether both figures represent packed, released, produced or sold mass, which periods they refer to and whether both combine green and golden tea. Keep the historical figures visible alongside separately labeled simulated output.

Collect operating days, shift hours, product mix, batch loads, cycle times, queue delays, changeovers, staffing, downtime, yield and packaging rejects for each era. Record the source and confidence of each value. Ask for production logs or dispatch records if available.

## Build a capacity model

Use accepted packed kilograms as the provisional simulation metric. Track fresh input, mass leaving each stage, moisture-related mass loss, scrap and held stock. Define stage load as batches or packs multiplied by occupied cycle time. Compute available hours from the operating calendar after planned downtime and track unplanned stops through run events. Flag any resource where demand exceeds available hours or buffers overflow.

Use variable output and near-daily operation in active months. Choose an independent calendar and demand scenario for the manual configuration. Replace those assumptions with actual calendars when known. Build fixtures for a single-product manual run, a mixed automated run and an adverse month.

## Explain the gain

Compare baseline and upgraded configurations with the same calendar, input availability and product mix first. Then use historically appropriate settings once available. Isolate changes in batch size, handling time, overlap, availability and yield one at a time. Record interactions separately so individually measured effects are not automatically added. Report the bottleneck before and after every modeled change.

## Test and report

Sweep downtime, product mix, cycle time and scrap. Show an output range when inputs are uncertain. Require a mass-balance ledger, station utilization, queue time, rejected mass and actual versus simulated output. Validate that released output excludes held or rejected stock. Publish a side-by-side monthly replay and a downloadable spreadsheet-ready CSV of assumptions and results.



## DAY-40 integration test and capacity replay

Use the variable 994 kg synthetic active month with 29 operating days. Use DAY-40 separately as an integration test with 30 kg green and 10 kg golden tea. Treat these values as editable assumptions. Calculate fresh leaf by product from net finished demand divided by assumed yield, and size station workload from occupied time plus changeovers.

Replay the manual baseline, drum addition, automated drying, machine rolling and proposed coordinated line. Run a common order book and report released kg, labor hours/kg, queue time, utilization, late orders and cost/kg. Keep founder-reported 100 and 1,000 kg/month separate from simulation results.



## Current operating baseline and portfolio responsibilities

The portfolio has 16 standalone projects plus the integrated factory capstone (Project 14), for 17 total. The seasonal operating baseline is near-daily during active months, with roughly 1,000 kg total per active month, variable daily output, golden tea only when eligible leaf is available, and zero fresh-leaf processing in January–February. Winter packing and shipping draw from released stock. Retail uses 50 g and 100 g bottles. DAY-40 is one synthetic integration test (30 kg green, 10 kg golden; 36 kg bulk and 4 kg retail in 40 × 50 g and 20 × 100 g bottles). MONTH-VAR is a separate synthetic month (994 kg over 29 operating days).

Operations owns the capacity and seasonal shifts model. Publish machine availability, reservations, worker assignments, queue timestamps and outage windows. For Project 17, replay the same orders and deliveries with revised delivery slots or shift coverage, then compare spoilage, overtime, service and energy. Keep the four staffing × winter-stock conference comparisons independently reproducible.
